## What was built

### Summary

Decisions are approved from mull serve: in Needs you, a group's Approve all (Approve for a group of one) and each decision's own Approve save through POST …/ops as the human, and what was approved leaves Needs you without being clouded. This sets the second kind of action, the write, in DESIGN.md: a heavier box and a tick, a second click to confirm, a hatched pending state, a NOT SAVED line in place on failure, and a redraw with a toast on a conflict.

### UI tour

1. Open a project with decisions in Needs you (tier 06, Decisions to approve) and open a group.
2. Click Approve all: it turns ink and asks "Approve 3?"; press Esc and it goes back.
3. Click Approve all twice: it says Saving…, a toast says Approved 3 decisions., and the group leaves Needs you, with no cloud for it.
4. Hover a decision in a group of two or more: its own Approve appears; approve one the same way, and only that one goes.
5. Mark revision seen still clears every other cloud; mull decisions shows the approvals by your git user.name with human true.

## How it was built

### Design notes

How: write.js holds the write kind. writeButton() draws a .btn.wr from a key, its batch ops and three labels (label, ask once armed, the toast once done); its state (armed, pending, error) lives in a map by key, so live re-renders from the event stream redraw it as it was. failure() draws the NOT SAVED line. app.js's write() runs the button: first click arms (a 4 s timer, Esc or any other click disarms), second sends api.ops(pid, the revision tip the page shows, ops). Success toasts and reloads the project; a 409 conflict drops the state, toasts that nothing was saved, and reloads; any other error stays on the button's failure line. focusKey knows data-write, so focus survives the re-render.

needs.js: group() uses writeButton for Approve all (Approve for a group of one) and decision() adds a per-row Approve in groups of two or more; review-carried groups keep Review only.

CSS: .btn.wr (1.5px border, tick), .armed (ink fill), .pending (hatch), .wfail; row approves hidden by opacity under hover:hover and shown on li hover/focus-within. While checking screenshots I found the chevron rotation rules matched every svg in a summary, which rotated the tick; they now target svg.ic, the chevron's class.

Docs: DESIGN.md gains the Two Kinds of Action Rule and replaces read-first in the overview, buttons, decision tier and Do's; serve/README, README and the model sheet say approving saves.

Checked in headless Chromium against a built server on a scratch repo: arm, pending, toast, the group leaving Needs you; new decisions arriving clouded and only the approved one's cloud going; a stale page getting the conflict toast; the failure line (user.name blanked) in light and dark.

### Decisions made in the session

Accepting the review ratifies these. To decide one differently, before accepting: `mull decide <id> "..."`

- **184c** Write buttons are a plain .btn with a 1.5px ink border and a leading tick; armed they fill with ink and ask; pending they're hatched in construction line; no new hue
  Why: DESIGN.md says a new state takes ink, grey or a line style before a hue. The hatch reuses the in-review cells' mark for work in progress.
- **2592** Groups carried by an open review keep only Review, with no per-decision approve
  Why: They're settled by reviewing the item; approving them one by one belongs with the review work in Settling the inbox.
- **388b** Confirming is a second click on the same button (armed for 4 s; Esc or a click elsewhere cancels), not a dialog
  Why: The phase doc asks for a light confirmation in place.
- **74e9** A failed write shows an inverted ink NOT SAVED label and the reason under what failed, kept across live re-renders until it's tried again; never red
  Why: Red is the revision cloud's alone. Inverted ink is the strongest mark that isn't a hue.
- **bb3f** A group of one decision has a single Approve on its header and no row button; larger groups have Approve all plus a per-decision Approve shown on hover or focus (always on touch screens)
  Why: Two buttons doing the same thing on one decision was redundant, and a box on every row turned a group into a column of buttons. Opacity, not visibility, keeps hidden row buttons in the tab order.
